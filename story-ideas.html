The Story Behind Perfect Fit

Where the idea came from and what I hoped readers would see beyond the story.

Where the Idea Came From

Perfect Fit began with a desire to bring more awareness to a rare genetic condition called CDKL5.

CDKL5 can begin changing a family's life when a baby is only a few weeks old. There can be seizures, testing, diagnosis, developmental delays, therapies, doctor visits, medications, seizure videos, timing seizures, and sleepless nights.

But I didn't want readers to see only the medical condition. I wanted them to see the child.

Sam and Cynthia are a lesbian couple who both want to experience motherhood. They decide to take turns experiencing pregnancy. They draw straws, and Sam, who is very masculine, becomes pregnant first.

Sam's appearance and her desire to become a mother create criticism from people who believe she should behave differently. Some even suggest that she and Cynthia are being punished by God because they are a same-sex couple who want to build a family.

I wanted to explore what those words can do to people.

I also wanted to challenge the idea that people are somehow less deserving of love, family, or happiness because they are different from what someone else believes they should be.

What I Wanted to Say

At the heart of the story is something very simple: we are all human beings.

We all have human desires, hopes, needs, and dreams.

I wanted to show that people are not being punished by God simply because of who they are or because they want to love someone and build a family together.

Lucy, the child living with CDKL5, becomes much more than a diagnosis. She becomes a reminder that what the world may see as a limitation can also bring something unique and beautiful into people's lives.

Lucy has a soul.

She has something to offer.

She has a place in this world.

Looking Beyond What We See

One of the ideas I wanted readers to take away from Perfect Fit is that just because someone is different does not mean they have less to offer.

Sometimes our differences are exactly what allow us to bring something unique and special into the lives of others.

I wanted readers to look beyond disease, disability, and differences and see the person and the soul inside what may not appear to be a "perfect" body.

Perhaps that is part of what Perfect Fit really means.

Maybe a perfect fit isn't a perfect life. Maybe it isn't a perfect body. Maybe it is discovering that every person has a place where they belong.

— Mark
